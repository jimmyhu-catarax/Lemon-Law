I. PURPOSE OF THIS DECLARATION

This declaration documents a discrete vehicle failure and roadside assistance breakdown event occurring February 9–13,
2026. It is submitted in connection with Repair Order #370267 (Paramus Hyundai Service Center) and constitutes Consumer's
sworn chronological account of the complete electrical failure of the subject vehicle, the impound incident that resulted
therefrom, and the systematic failures of Hyundai Roadside Assistance that prolonged vehicle recovery across five calendar
days. This incident accounts for a portion of the 49+ additional days out of service accrued beginning February 9, 2026, and is
submitted to supplement the Repair Order documentation in Exhibit 04.

III. CHRONOLOGICAL NARRATIVE


Monday, February 9, 2026 (Reference Cases: #765-30936, #765-31242)
Consumer retrieved the subject vehicle (2024 Hyundai IONIQ 5, VIN KM8KNDDF9RU253237) from Mizzoni's Auto Body,
where a headlamp assembly repair had been completed. The vehicle was driven from the body shop to Consumer's office and
subsequently to 780 Main Avenue, Passaic, NJ. At approximately 12:45 PM, Consumer parked the vehicle in the lot across
from the KFC at that address. At approximately 1:00 PM — fifteen minutes after parking — Consumer returned to the vehicle
and found it completely inoperable.

    Failure Condition: Complete electrical failure. The vehicle would not start, the doors were unlocked (loss of security), and
    hazard/blinker lights were non-functional. Consumer immediately contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance to request a tow
    truck. Due to the vehicle's immobility and inability to wait, Consumer walked approximately 30 minutes back to the office to
    attend to patient care obligations.
    At 6:00 PM, Consumer contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance for a status update; no tow truck had arrived. Roadside
    Assistance agents acknowledged they had an incorrect callback number on file, which caused the initial response delay.
    While en route home via rideshare, Consumer contacted Roadside Assistance again and made alternative arrangements:
    Nancy, the KFC store manager at 780 Main Avenue, agreed to remain on-site to supervise the tow.
    Later that evening, an AAA/Hyundai-dispatched driver arrived at 780 Main Avenue and witnessed the subject vehicle being
    actively towed away by a separate, unauthorized tow truck. Consumer immediately contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance
    to report the unauthorized tow, and the vehicle was logged as 'missing.'
Tuesday–Wednesday, February 10–11, 2026 (Reference Case: #765-325-70)
Consumer spent February 10 through the morning of February 11 contacting and visiting local impound facilities in an effort to
locate the vehicle. At approximately 4:00 PM on February 11, Consumer located the vehicle at Renari's Towing Services (179
Main Avenue, Passaic, NJ). Consumer confirmed the vehicle was impounded and was quoted approximately $391.00 in
release fees, with additional fees possible. As the lot closed at 5:00 PM and an immediate tow could not be arranged,
Consumer contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance and was instructed to call back Thursday morning to coordinate vehicle
recovery.

Thursday, February 12, 2026 (Reference Cases: #765-329-99, #765-331-94)
Consumer arrived in person at Renari's Towing to release the vehicle. Consumer paid approximately $400.00 in impound
release fees and surrendered the vehicle key to the clerk, Ms. Sandra Elliott. Ms. Elliott provided a receipt and confirmed that:
(1) she would give the key to Renari's internal driver to ensure it remained with the vehicle; and (2) staff would be available to
supervise the Hyundai tow pickup.

    First Tow Attempt (Failed): Hyundai Roadside Assistance dispatched an AAA driver to Renari's business address (179
    Main Avenue). Ms. Elliott clarified that the physical vehicle was located at their service station at an alternate address. When
    the driver contacted Consumer to relay this information, the driver became verbally aggressive regarding the address
    discrepancy and refused to proceed. The driver hung up without resolving the situation.
    Second Tow Attempt (Failed): Consumer re-contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance, reported the driver misconduct, and
    provided explicit instructions including Ms. Elliott's direct contact number so that the next driver could coordinate directly with
    Renari's staff. A new ticket was opened and a second driver, Caesar, was dispatched. Hyundai Roadside Assistance failed
    to relay Ms. Elliott's contact information to Caesar. Caesar arrived at the lot, found no one present, had no contact number to
    call, and departed. The ticket was closed without recovery.
    Third Tow Attempt (Failed): At 4:00 PM, Consumer contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance a third time to reopen the
    request. The driver did not arrive until 5:40 PM — forty minutes after Renari's Towing closes at 5:00 PM. The facility was
    closed and the tow could not be completed.

Friday, February 13, 2026 (Reference Case: #765-33-647)
Consumer contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance first thing in the morning. A tow truck was successfully dispatched and the
vehicle was recovered from Renari's Towing. The vehicle was towed to Paramus Hyundai Service Center, 234 Route 4,
Paramus, NJ 07652, where it was admitted under Repair Order #370267 for battery failure diagnostics and repair. This
concluded a five-day period during which the vehicle was entirely out of Consumer's possession and control due to the
electrical failure and the cascade of Roadside Assistance failures described above.
