SECTION I — INCIDENT NARRATIVE (January 6 – February 20,
2026)


Purpose
This supplemental exhibit provides a complete factual record of the incidental damages sustained by
Claimant Dr. Jimmy Y. Hu, MD, as a direct and proximate result of (a) a manufacturing defect in the
high-voltage battery system of his 2024 Hyundai IONIQ 5 SEL AWD (VIN: KM8KNDDF9RU253237),
and (b) failures by Hyundai Roadside Assistance that materially compounded those damages, during
the period of January 6 through February 20, 2026. The complete itemization of all expenses described
below appears in Exhibit 17 (Itemized Incidental Expense Ledger), and the supporting receipts are
compiled in Exhibit 21 (Expense Receipt Packet). The total claimed is $1,989.25.

Chronological Incident Log
January 5, 2026
Vehicle driven without incident. Battery and EV systems appeared fully functional.
January 6, 2026 — First Battery Failure
On the morning of January 6, 2026, the high-voltage battery was completely dead. The vehicle would
not start, and it would not accept a charge when plugged into an EV charging port. Claimant attempted
both starting and charging the vehicle without success; contemporaneous video recordings of these
SUPPLEMENTAL EXHIBIT — Hu v. Hyundai Motor America | AG Case No. NC-1-1249605441

failed attempts exist and are available for the arbitrator's review. Claimant was forced to take a cab to
and from his ophthalmology practice in Clifton, NJ. Hyundai Roadside Assistance responded and
reportedly was able to jump-start the vehicle.
January 7, 2026 — Second Failure & Tow to Paramus Hyundai
The following morning, January 7, 2026, the vehicle was again completely dead. Claimant contacted
Hyundai Roadside Assistance, and the vehicle was towed to the Hyundai Paramus Service Center. An
EV technician examined the vehicle and raised the concern that the failure could be due either to a
manufacturing defect or to the pre-existing damage on the right headlamp assembly. Claimant
regarded the headlamp theory as unlikely: that damage had been present since December 22, 2024,
and was never flagged as mechanically relevant when Hyundai returned the vehicle to Claimant on
August 7, 2025 after its prior 126-day battery-defect repair. The Hyundai Paramus Service Center
wanted Claimant to front the cost of diagnostic testing.
January 2026 — Headlamp Blame Amid Declining Settlement Pressure
While the Paramus dealership advanced the headlamp theory and sought to have Claimant front the
cost of diagnostic testing, Hyundai Motor America was simultaneously asserting that the failure was a
headlamp issue rather than a warranty-covered manufacturing defect — while also pressing Claimant
to accept a declining Lemon Law settlement offer. The juxtaposition of these two postures —
disclaiming the warranty defect on one hand while pushing a declining settlement on the other — is
itself probative of the Respondent's posture toward this claim.
January 8–23, 2026 — Mounting Loss-of-Use Costs
While the vehicle sat at the Paramus Hyundai Service Center and Claimant weighed the diagnostic-fee
request, he had no functional vehicle and continued incurring out-of-pocket transportation costs. He
rented a Hyundai Kona from National Car Rental (Jan 8–16, $412.57), then a vehicle from Avis (Jan
20–23, $192.09), supplemented by Lyft rides and public transit.
January 22, 2026 — Headlamp Replaced at Mizzoni's Auto Body (Self-Pay)
Claimant elected to replace the right headlamp assembly at Mizzoni's Auto Body, authorizing a "Self-
Pay" repair to bypass insurance and warranty delays. This also had the effect of eliminating the only
alternative explanation Hyundai had offered: with the headlamp now addressed, any subsequent failure
would necessarily point to the battery defect. The vehicle was stored in an indoor garage for
approximately two weeks following the repair.
February 9, 2026 — The Headlamp Theory Collapses at the KFC Lot
On February 9, 2026, Claimant retrieved the vehicle from Mizzoni's Auto Body with the headlamp repair
confirmed complete. He drove the vehicle approximately 20 minutes; the high-voltage battery displayed
35–40% state of charge with roughly 130 miles of estimated range. At approximately 12:45 PM,
Claimant parked at 780 Main Avenue, Passaic, NJ (a lot across from a KFC). At approximately 1:00 PM
— fifteen minutes later — the vehicle was completely dead: no start, no hazard lights, and doors that
would not lock. This was the factual checkmate: with the headlamp now fully replaced, the battery failed
again, conclusively demonstrating that the headlamp had never been the cause. Hyundai Roadside
Assistance was contacted (Reference Case #76530936).
February 9–12, 2026 — Roadside Dispatch Failures & Unauthorized Impound
Claimant contacted Hyundai Roadside Assistance at approximately 1:00 PM to request a tow
(Reference Cases #765-30936 and #765-31242). Because the vehicle was immobilized, Claimant
walked roughly 30 minutes back to the office to attend to patients. By 6:00 PM no tow truck had arrived;
Claimant called Roadside Assistance for a status update, and called again while traveling home that
evening. Roadside Assistance stated that it had attempted to reach Claimant but had the wrong
callback number on file, and that it required either the key to be left with the vehicle or a person present
to supervise the tow. As Claimant was at a work event and unable to return, he arranged for Nancy, the
KFC store manager at 780 Main Avenue, to supervise the tow on site. When the AAA driver dispatched
by Hyundai arrived that night, he witnessed the vehicle being actively towed away by a different,

unauthorized tow truck. Claimant reported the unauthorized tow to Hyundai Roadside Assistance, and
the vehicle was filed as "missing."
On February 10 and 11, Claimant searched local impound lots to locate the vehicle. At approximately
4:00 PM on February 11 (Reference Case #765-325-70), he located it at Raineri's Towing, confirmed
impounded, with release fees quoted at approximately $391 plus potential additional charges. Because
the lot closed at 5:00 PM, an immediate release was impossible, and Roadside Assistance instructed
Claimant to call back the following morning to arrange the tow.
On February 12 (Reference Cases #765-329-99 and #765-331-94), Claimant went in person to
Raineri's Towing at 179 Main Avenue, paid in excess of $400 in release fees, and handed the key to
the clerk, Ms. Sandra Elliott, who confirmed she would give it to the lot's internal driver and have staff
supervise the Hyundai pickup. Three successive Hyundai-dispatched tow attempts then failed: (1) the
first AAA driver was sent to the office address rather than the service-station address where the vehicle
was held, reacted with verbal hostility, and hung up; (2) Roadside Assistance failed to pass Ms. Elliott's
coordinating phone number to the next driver, Caesar, who arrived to find no one present, had no
number to call, and left — after which the ticket was closed; and (3) a third driver, requested at 4:00
PM, did not arrive until 5:40 PM, after the lot had already closed at 5:00 PM. Each of these failures is
directly attributable to Hyundai Roadside Assistance and compounded Claimant's loss of use and
impound exposure.
February 10–20, 2026 — Second National Rental
To maintain access to his surgical practice while the vehicle was held, Claimant rented a Chevrolet
Trax from National Car Rental (Feb 10–20, $608.40), held through the date the vehicle was returned.
February 13, 2026 — Vehicle Arrives at Paramus Hyundai; $540 Diagnostic Fee Requested
On the morning of February 13 (Reference Case #765-33-647), Hyundai Roadside Assistance finally
dispatched a successful tow; the vehicle was recovered from the impound lot and transported to the
Hyundai Paramus Service Center. The dealership requested a $540 diagnostic fee as a precondition to
inspecting the vehicle.
February 18, 2026 (Approx.) — Diagnostic Agreement Executed
Claimant executed the Hyundai Service Diagnostic Agreement for the $540 fee. Paramus Hyundai
service staff verbally stated that the fee would be waived if the defect proved to be an issue covered
under the manufacturer warranty.
February 19, 2026 — Warranty Defect Confirmed
Paramus Hyundai completed diagnostics and officially confirmed that the root cause is a defect covered
under the manufacturer warranty, and authorized replacement of the high-voltage battery. Because the
defect was warranty-covered, the $540 diagnostic fee was waived per the parties' verbal agreement.
This is the same core battery/EV system defect that caused the vehicle's original 126-day loss-of-use
period beginning April 3, 2025.
February 20, 2026 — Vehicle Returned
Vehicle was returned to Claimant on February 20, 2026. Claimant used a final Lyft ride ($83.92) from
the rental return point to Paramus Hyundai to retrieve the vehicle.
