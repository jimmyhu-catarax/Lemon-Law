## What the secondary sources indicate

| Point | NY (GBL § 198-a) | NJ (N.J.S.A. 56:12-29 et seq.) |
|---|---|---|
| Days out of service threshold | 30 | **20** |
| Repair-attempt threshold | 4 | 3 |
| Coverage trigger | leased in NY | **"registered, sold or leased in the State of New Jersey, whether purchased, leased or repaired in the State or outside the State"** |
| Term of protection | 18,000 mi / 2 yrs | **24,000 mi or 2 yrs from original delivery, whichever is earlier** |

**Fit against our facts (all four figures are already source-verified):**

- **RO #358530 = 124 days** — **6.2×** the NJ 20-day threshold (vs 4.1× in NY).
- Registered in NJ ✓ (arbitration form Q7 answers "registered in New York: No").
- All service at Hyundai of Paramus, NJ ✓.
- Delivery 5/22–23/2024 → the two-year term of protection closed **≈ May 2026**.
  Every nonconformity was **reported inside it**: 2/12/2025 (7,026 mi),
  4/3/2025 (8,148 mi), 2/18/2026 (11,230 mi) — and all well under 24,000 mi.

**Procedural routes indicated:** NJ Division of Consumer Affairs Lemon Law Unit
(hearing before an OAL administrative law judge), the manufacturer's informal
program, or a civil action. One source states a consumer is eligible for
Division dispute resolution **only once** per vehicle.

## THE TWO QUESTIONS THAT PROBABLY DECIDE IT — both unresolved

1. **Preclusion.** Does losing the NY compulsory arbitration bar a NJ claim on
   the same nonconformity — res judicata, collateral estoppel, or an
   election-of-remedies provision in the NJ statute? **This is the threshold
   question and nothing was found on it.**
2. **Limitations period.** The 24,000-mile / two-year window is the *term of
   protection* (when the defect must be reported), which is **not** the same as
   the deadline to bring an action. One source suggested the UCC four-year
   period may apply; another implied two years from delivery. **Unresolved, and
   dispositive if it is the shorter reading** — two years from delivery expired
   ≈ May 2026.

## Why it would matter if it survives

A NJ claim would be a **direct claim on the merits**, not an Article 75 petition
to vacate. That is a different and better posture: no deference to an
arbitrator, no need to prove the award was irrational, and a threshold the
record clears by 6.2×. It would also change the economics Krukas flagged —
a direct claim is the kind of case he said he would "have zero doubt" about.
